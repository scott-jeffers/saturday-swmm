Model storm sewers on your own computer.

Open a SWMM model, edit the network on a map, design a storm and run it with OpenSWMM 6 or EPA SWMM 5. Then watch where the pipes surcharge and the streets flood. Free, open source, and nothing to install.

Download for Windows View the source

Portable zip for Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit). Mac and Linux users can run it from source.

Two SWMM engines, one model

Pick the engine per project and switch any time. Runs, live map colouring, time series and profiles work the same with each, so you can check one engine's results against the other.

EngineWhat it isUse it for
OpenSWMM 6Default The community continuation of EPA SWMM by Caleb Buahin (HydroCouple), with a built-in 2D overland solver. Pipe networks with 2D surface flooding on a mesh.
EPA SWMM 5 The reference EPA SWMM 5.2 engine, run through PySWMM. Checking results against the engine most standards are written around, and models built for SWMM 5.
Flow accumulation A terrain analysis of your DEM. No SWMM run. Quick floodplain screening before a model exists.

Running in a minute

  1. Download and unzip

    Unzip the folder anywhere you can write to, such as Documents. It carries its own Python, so it won't touch anything already on your computer.

  2. Double-click Saturday-SWMM.cmd

    Your browser opens the app at 127.0.0.1. Keep the small console window open while you work.

  3. Open your project folder

    Any folder with a SWMM .inp file. Results are written next to it as the usual .rpt and .out.

What you can do with it

Edit the network on a map

Junctions, conduits, storage, outfalls and subcatchments over OpenStreetMap or aerial imagery. Drag nodes, draw new links, edit attributes, undo.

Design storms

NOAA Atlas 14 depths, NRCS and Huff distributions, TR-55, or real rainfall from Open-Meteo, written straight into the model as a rain gauge.

Watch the run

Pipes and manholes colour by flow and depth while the simulation runs. Cancel at any point.

Read the results

Scrub through time on the map, pick two manholes for a long-section, chart any node, link or subcatchment, and open the report.

See 2D flooding

Generate a surface mesh from a DEM and play back flood depth across the streets, rendered with WebGL for large meshes.

Bring your GIS

Add GeoTIFF DEMs and shapefiles as background layers, in any coordinate system your project uses.

Your models stay on your machine

Saturday-SWMM is a web app that runs locally. The engine, your .inp files and every result stay on your computer; the server only answers to that computer. There are no accounts, no uploads and no usage tracking. The only things fetched from the internet are map tiles, storm data you ask for, and the update check.

It's free software under the GPL-3.0, and every release is built from tagged source with pinned dependencies, so you can see exactly what you are running.
