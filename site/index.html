Open, edit and run SWMM models

Saturday-SWMM shows a SWMM .inp on a map, runs it with OpenSWMM 6 or EPA SWMM 5, and reads back the .rpt and .out: flooding, surcharge, depths and flows by node and link. Scenarios are ordinary .inp files. It is open source under the GPL and needs no installation.

Two SWMM engines, one model

Saturday-SWMM picks the engine for each model, and you can switch any time. Runs, live map colouring, time series and profiles work the same with each, so you can check one engine's results against the other.

EngineWhat it isUse it for
EPA SWMM 5.2Default for 1D models The reference EPA SWMM engine, run through PySWMM on your computer, or compiled for the browser in the online viewer. Design and permit work: it is the engine most standards are written around.
OpenSWMM 6Used for 2D models The community continuation of EPA SWMM by Caleb Buahin (HydroCouple), with a built-in 2D overland solver. Still a pre-release. Pipe networks with 2D surface flooding on a mesh.

Or look at a model without installing anything

The browser viewer opens an .inp with its .rpt and .out from any SWMM 5 or OpenSWMM run: the network on a map, properties, report tables, peak colours, charts, profiles and run comparisons. You can edit the model and run it there too, with EPA SWMM 5.2 compiled to WebAssembly. The files stay in your browser and are never uploaded. Scenarios, design storms and 2D flooding need the download.

Running in a minute

  1. Download and unzip

    Unzip the folder anywhere you can write to, such as Documents. It carries its own Python, so it won't touch anything already on your computer.

  2. Double-click Saturday-SWMM.cmd

    The app opens in your browser on its own. Keep the small console window open while you work; closing it, or Quit in the app, stops it.

  3. Open your project folder

    Any folder with a SWMM .inp file, or one of the bundled examples. Results are written next to it as the usual .rpt and .out.

What you can do with it

Edit the network on a map

Junctions, conduits, storage, outfalls and subcatchments over OpenStreetMap or aerial imagery. Drag nodes, draw links, select many at once, right-click for what you need next, and undo any change.

Every section, like a spreadsheet

Curves, time series, patterns, inflows, dry-weather flow, controls and the rest of the .inp as editable tables. Paste straight from Excel, and set every simulation option in one place.

Scenarios that stay SWMM

Each what-if is a complete, standard .inp you can open in any SWMM tool. Edits to a parent reach every scenario below it, field by field, and each scenario's own changes are kept and marked.

Design storms

NOAA Atlas 14 depths, NRCS and Huff distributions, TR-55, or real rainfall from Open-Meteo, written straight into the model as a rain gauge.

Check, then run

The model is checked for missing nodes, cross-sections, disconnected pieces and uphill pipes before every run. Pipes colour by flow and depth as it runs.

Read the results

A run summary with flooding, surcharge and continuity, report tables linked to the map, profiles from any manhole to its outfall with the highest water level of the run, and charts of any node, link or subcatchment.

Compare runs

Every run is kept by engine and scenario. Compare two to see the change in flood volume and the pipes that changed most, overlay their hydrographs with your observed data, and export CSV or GeoJSON.

See 2D flooding

Generate a surface mesh from a DEM and play back flood depth across the streets, rendered with WebGL for large meshes.

Bring your GIS

Add GeoTIFF DEMs and shapefiles as background layers, in any coordinate system your project uses.

Your models stay on your machine

Saturday-SWMM is a web app that runs locally. The engine, your .inp files and every result stay on your computer; the server only answers to that computer. There are no accounts, no uploads and no usage tracking. The only things fetched from the internet are map tiles, storm data you ask for, and the update check.

The app tells you when a newer Saturday-SWMM, OpenSWMM or EPA SWMM engine is out. One click installs it, and one click undoes it if a model behaves differently.

It's free software under the GPL-3.0, and every release is built from tagged source with pinned dependencies, so you can see exactly what you are running.
